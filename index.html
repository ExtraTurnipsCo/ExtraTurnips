Why I stopped checking my phone first thing in the morning

May 22, 2026 · 4 min read habits

For years, my alarm went off and within thirty seconds I was scrolling. Emails, headlines, whatever showed up. I told myself it was just staying informed.

Then one morning I put my phone across the room before bed, almost by accident. When I woke up, I had to actually get up to turn the alarm off. By that point I was standing, which meant I was awake, which meant the scrolling window had closed.

That one small friction changed everything. My mornings got quieter. I started noticing what I actually wanted to do — make coffee, sit by the window, think about the day ahead — instead of reacting to whatever the internet handed me first.

I'm not saying phones are bad. I'm just saying: the first thirty minutes of your day are yours. You don't have to give them away.

The cookbook I keep going back to

May 10, 2026 · 3 min read food

I own a lot of cookbooks. Most of them are beautiful objects that I flip through once and shelve. But there's one that's held together with a rubber band now because I've used it so much.

What makes it different isn't the recipes — it's that it explains the why. Why you salt pasta water, why you let meat rest, why high heat matters for a sear. Once you understand the principles, you stop needing to follow recipes so carefully.

The best cooking knowledge isn't a list of dishes. It's a way of thinking about food. Find a book that teaches you that, and you'll use it forever.

Notes from a week without social media

April 28, 2026 · 5 min read reflection

I didn't announce it or make it a thing. I just deleted the apps on a Sunday and decided to see what a week felt like without them.

The first two days were weird. I kept picking up my phone and having nothing to do with it. I noticed how many times a day I reached for it out of pure habit — not boredom, not curiosity, just reflex.

By day four something shifted. I started finishing things. Books, tasks, conversations. The low-level background noise I didn't know I had went quiet.

I went back to social media after the week. But I went back differently — less automatically, more deliberately. That felt like enough of a win.
